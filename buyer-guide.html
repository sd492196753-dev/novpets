SAMPLE APPROVAL

A sample review that produces a usable specification.

Use a physical sample and a written checklist together. The aim is to agree what will be supplied and what evidence supports any claim.

  1. Record dimensions and installation

    Measure panels, straps and attachments in millimetres. For a seat cover, note headrest configuration and seat-belt access. For a boot liner, record floor width at the wheel arches, depth and coverage of side walls or seat backs. Keep photographs of the fitted sample with the vehicle or test setup identified.

  2. Compare materials and cleaning claims

    List surface fabric, backing, padding, seams and hardware. Request the proposed cleaning method and check a sample after cleaning under those instructions. Ask which exact model, component and conditions support water-resistance, colourfastness or durability claims. Do not transfer a fabric-level claim to the finished product without evidence.

  3. Check the variant and component mix

    For sets, build a matrix of size, colour and matching components. Record quantities per combination and how each variation is labelled. Ask whether minimum quantities apply per product, component, size or colour rather than only to the combined order.

  4. Measure the packed product

    Record retail-pack dimensions, gross weight, units per carton and master-carton dimensions. Check barcode position, carton marks, care instructions and the retailer’s packaging requirements. Use packed measurements, not unfolded product dimensions, for freight and warehouse planning.

  5. Keep an approval reference

    Save the sample identifier, dated specification, logo artwork, colour references and packaging approval. Agree inspection points and tolerances, including the process for recording discrepancies. A change to material, hardware or artwork should be reviewed before production continues.

  6. Compare a complete quotation

    Use the same currency, variant quantities, Incoterm and destination for supplier comparisons. Separate sample charges, setup, unit price, packaging, freight, import costs and local handling. Confirm the payment stages and when the quoted production lead time starts.

These are purchasing checks, not product test results or an assurance of compliance. Seat covers and liners protect interiors; they are not vehicle restraints.

ANSWERS FOR BUSINESS BUYERS

Buyer guide FAQS

What is the difference between a dog seat cover and a boot liner?+

A seat cover protects the rear-seat interior; a boot or cargo liner protects the vehicle load area. Their measurements, coverage and fixing points differ. Choose the use area first, then compare the specification and sample for that area.

How do I compare wholesale dog travel suppliers?+

Compare written specifications, physical samples, the quantity per variant, packing data and quotation terms. Check what evidence supports performance claims and what happens if production differs from the approved sample. A low unit price alone does not establish the landed cost or suitability.

What is needed to start an enquiry with NOV PETS?+
