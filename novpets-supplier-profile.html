WHOLESALE BUYER NOTES

When does a sourcing partner fit your buying brief?

A branded collection

Consider a sourcing discussion when your business needs coordinated products, logos or packaging and can approve specifications and samples before a bulk order. Start with the selected range and develop the size, colour and packaging mix together.

For a complete request, use the wholesale buyer guide and order-route comparison. A sourcing partner, an owned factory and a local stock distributor are different supply models; check which responsibilities your quotation covers.

Orders with a specific deadline

If immediate local dispatch is essential, verify local stock with the supplier offering it. For an overseas order, confirm sample approval, production starting conditions, dispatch and transit separately. A target arrival date needs an agreed order and shipping plan.

For destination planning, consult the Australia buying checklist or New Zealand buying checklist.

WHOLESALE BUYER NOTES

Confirm the evidence for your selected product.

A useful supplier comparison rests on a product specification and agreed order terms. Ask for the documents and samples that address your actual buying risk.

  1. Product specification

    Record measurements, layers and materials, fittings, component list, cleaning instructions and packaging. Review claims such as waterproofing, washability and vehicle fit against the selected sample.

  2. Sample and quality agreement

    Keep the approved sample reference, artwork, acceptance criteria and handling of discrepancies with the purchase order. Ask for evidence for any certification or safety claim before using it in a retail listing.

  3. Commercial and importing terms

    Agree seller details, price and currency, MOQ per variant, payment milestones, dispatch location, Incoterm and named place. Confirm the importing responsibilities and required documents for Australia or New Zealand.

Concept images, illustrative capacity figures and sample testimonials on this website are design material, not proof of product performance, customer orders or owned manufacturing facilities. Confirm supplier identity and product-specific evidence before placing an order.
